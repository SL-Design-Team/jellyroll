The enterprise-software defaults for numbers, dates, times, and units, taken from the Microsoft Writing Style Guide. Where we add to it for a data-heavy product (ISO 8601, relative time, KPI abbreviations), the rule says so.

Numerals and words

We follow the Microsoft Writing Style Guide. In UI, the product should format numbers, dates, and times for the person’s locale automatically. This page covers the text we write ourselves.

In UI text, use numerals for every number
Labels, counts, badges, and tables use numerals even for zero through nine. Example: 3 pipelines selected.
In running text, spell out zero through nine
Use numerals for 10 and greater. Example: five connections, 12 connections.
Always use numerals for these
Measurements (3 GB, 250 ms), values someone is told to enter, percentages, time of day, dimensions, and step or version numbers.
Be consistent in a group
If one item needs a numeral, use numerals for every item of that type.
Don’t start a sentence with a numeral
Rewrite it, or spell the number out. More than 10 pipelines ran.
Spell out ordinals
the first row. Never firstly or secondly.

Formatting numbers

Use commas in numbers of four or more digits
1,024. Exceptions: years, pixel sizes, page numbers, and addresses take a comma only at five or more digits, if at all. Never use a comma after the decimal point.
Add a zero before a decimal point
0.5 cm. Align decimals on the point in tables.
Use a real minus sign for negative numbers
−79, not an en dash or a hyphen.
Use from and through for ranges in prose
from 9 through 17. Use an en dash in tables and tight UI: 2016–2020. Use to for times: 10:00 AM to 2:00 PM.
Spell out thousand, million, and billion in prose
$30 million. In KPI tiles and chart axes, where space is tight, 1.2M is acceptable: capitalize K, M, and B, use no space, and give the full value in a tooltip or accessible name.
Percentages: a numeral and the word in prose, the symbol in UI
5 percent in sentences, 42% in tables, tiles, and charts.
Use a space between a number and its unit
5 GB, 250 ms, 3 cm. Choose one convention for data sizes on a surface and keep it.
Use hyphens in phone numbers
612-555-0175
Don’t type numbers that the product can format
Locale decides the thousands and decimal separators. Hand-typed separators in a string can be wrong for a reader.

Dates and times

Write dates as month, day, year
October 1, 2026. Spell out the month so no one has to guess whether 6/12 means June or December. Never use ordinals: June 1, not June 1st.
Use ISO 8601 where the value is data
Logs, exports, API fields, and code-facing inputs use 2026-10-01T14:30:00Z. Write the friendly format for people and ISO for machines.
Write the time with AM or PM
10:45 AM, 6:30 PM. Capitalize both letters and add a space. Use noon and midnight, never 12:00.
Say which time zone when it matters
Include it when people in other zones might see the time. Write UTC, never GMT, and use an offset such as Eastern Time (UTC−5). Show timestamps in the person’s local time, and make the zone available.
Use relative time for recent events
5 minutes ago for the last day, then an absolute date. Always give the exact timestamp on hover or focus.
Use a time range with to in text
10:00 AM to 2:00 PM. Use an en dash with no spaces in schedules and listings: 10:00 AM–2:00 PM.
Capitalize days and months, and abbreviate only when space is tight
Three letters, no period: Mon, Jan. Calendars and date pickers can use two letters or one.
Avoid seasons and 24/7
Use a month or quarter instead of a season. Use always or all day, every day instead of 24/7.

Counts, plurals, and currency

Match the count
1 pipeline, 2 pipelines. Never pipeline(s); it can’t be translated correctly.
Say No in prose, 0 in counters
No pipelines match your filters. In a tab badge or table, a plain 0.
Show progress as of
3 of 12 selected
Put the currency symbol before the amount
$1,200.00. When more than one currency can appear on a screen, use the ISO code so none is ambiguous: USD 1,200.00.
